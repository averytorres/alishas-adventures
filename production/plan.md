# Alisha and the Lost Star — production pipeline

Status: production specification, 2026-11-04. This document does not mark any art, animation, narration, or Android behavior as finished.

## Product contract

- Layout and Android play are portrait only. The app should not rotate into a landscape game layout; desktop preview stays in a narrow portrait-width column.

- Audience: English-speaking children around age four learning English, Spanish, or Gujarati. Parent chooses language and one of two Alisha designs: **Adventurer** or **Star**. No French in the full game.
- App opens to a scrollable story library. Each card opens on tap; holding its picture or tapping its separate speaker button reads a short teaser. A visible and spoken instruction teaches this interaction. Parent settings and Android app pinning live behind the parent gate.
- Each story screen speaks the situation, then each choice in order, highlighting the phrase currently being spoken. Every line visible to the child has corresponding recorded speech in each supported language. Tap once to select. Hold a choice or tap its separate speaker button to repeat that choice. A Replay button rereads the current situation and choices without changing progress. Starting any playback cancels the previous playback cleanly. Help gives a short, scene-specific spoken hint without selecting a choice.
- Story is CYOA: two choices on most screens, occasionally three, no single-Continue screens or separate mini-games. A route takes about 10–15 minutes; the full graph has multiple substantially different routes and endings, mostly positive with gentle setbacks and an immediate route back into play. Save progress per story. No path should rely on reading skill to understand a choice.
- The first production story is **Alisha and the Lost Star**. Later stories use the same player and asset contracts. Story graph and English script should be reviewed before translation, illustration, or paid voice generation.

## Current baseline and gap

`work/cyoa` currently has a short one-story prototype: two single-image princess portraits, whole-image idle motion, generated music/effects, English/Spanish/French copy, three endings, and a Java WebView Android wrapper with a parent gate and screen-pinning bridge. It lacks a story library, Gujarati, spoken narration, timed highlighting, layered characters, local voice assets, and the final art pipeline. Its old tests cover only the prototype paths. `build-android.ps1` and the local Unity-provided Android SDK/JDK exist; an APK already exists, but that is not evidence of the planned experience running on a phone.

## 1. Story and localization source

Use one versioned story manifest per adventure. Stable IDs are shared across languages and must not change when prose is revised. For every scene record: `id`, `backgroundId`, character placements/expressions/gestures, situation line IDs, 2–3 choice IDs with targets, help line IDs, and ending metadata if applicable. Separate localized string tables under `en`, `es`, `gu`; never copy a missing language from English at runtime. Teasers, library instructions, controls, help, ending text, parent-facing copy, and accessibility labels also require approved locale entries where the child hears or sees them. Parent setup may remain English by product decision, but label that choice explicitly.

Write a graph inventory before assets: list every node and edge, route durations at expected narration speed, shared/reconverging scenes, ending types, and a route coverage matrix. A choice must lead to a distinct scene or materially different consequence; review reconvergences for false choices. Give an English-speaking four-year-old enough visual context and repeated vocabulary to follow the selected language. The user cannot review Spanish or Gujarati, so use independent translation passes, line-by-line comparison with English, targeted dictionary checks, and voice-sample listening. A fluent human review would improve confidence if it later becomes available, but its absence does not stop development; mark the language-quality limitation honestly. Gujarati text requires a font with tested Gujarati shaping in Android WebView; include its license and test it offline on target devices.

## 2. Character and scene art

Create a shared Alisha rig specification for the **Adventurer** and **Star** designs, while preserving each design's silhouette, palette, costume, and face. Keep editable layered masters (for example PSD/ORA/other exportable layered source) and export transparent, trimmed PNG or WebP layers plus a machine-readable atlas/rig manifest. Use consistent anchors and canvas scale so both designs can use the same motion code. Minimum independent layers: rear hair, body/costume, head/face, eye-open and eye-closed (plus a partial lid if useful), brows, mouth shapes, near/far upper arms and forearms/hands, front hair, and accessories. Separate the crown/accessory where motion or occlusion requires it. Avoid a cutout look by drawing overlap beneath joints and checking seams at full arm rotation.

Required reusable poses/actions for **both** princesses: neutral idle with subtle breathing and weight shift; natural unsynchronized blink; speech mouth states (rest, open/narrow, wide smile/rounded) driven by audio timing; looking/listening reaction; one-hand point; welcoming/open-arm gesture; concerned/thinking gesture; small celebratory gesture. Provide left/right variants where mirroring would flip costume details or lighting incorrectly. Use short transitions and return to idle without jumps. The mouth and eyes must be able to animate simultaneously with the arms; blinking must not pause speech. No speech should be represented merely by whole-portrait bobbing. Keep eyes and mouth readable at actual phone size.

Scene art should be layered into background, midground, foreground, and optional atmosphere/effect planes, with safe areas for the story panel and small-screen cropping. Start with a visual inventory from the approved graph: castle, forest, sky, dragon, star, and only the scene variants actually required. The dragon needs its own expression/gesture plan if it speaks or conveys story-critical emotion. Keep illustration source, exported layers, scene ID, revision, color profile, dimensions, and license/provenance together. Set a phone pixel/memory budget after profiling a representative device; do not promise a fixed resolution before composition tests. Review portraits and scenes side by side in both princess modes, portrait phone layout, low brightness, and reduced-motion mode.

## 3. Narration and timing

Use exactly one woman narrator for each language: one English, one Spanish, and one Gujarati voice. There is no child-facing voice picker or three-voice Alphabert-style pack.

Use **approved, prerecorded, local** audio for English, Spanish, and Gujarati, including teaser, instruction, situation, each choice, help, and endings. Do not use device TTS as a silent fallback for missing production audio. Choose voices through short samples in all three languages and review them as carefully as available language knowledge permits; document that fluent human listening was unavailable. ElevenLabs model/voice capabilities and credit rates must be checked against the account before generation; Gujarati support and pronunciation must be tested with a small sample before committing to a voice. Do not store API keys in the app or repository.

Production order: lock English lines and IDs -> approve translations -> normalize punctuation/proper-name pronunciation -> generate a **small** sample set -> listen and revise -> count/approve full batch -> generate unique lines once per language -> QC audio and timing -> package. Keep source text, translated text, voice/model/settings, audio hash, generation date, reviewer, and revision for each line. Do not regenerate unchanged shared lines. Record explicit silence at start/end only if needed for clean playback; normalize loudness consistently and check clipping/noise.

For highlighting, each line has an audio file and ordered word or short-phrase cue intervals in milliseconds (`startMs`, `endMs`, `textRange` or stable token IDs). Generate initial cues with forced alignment where the language is supported, then review manually, especially Gujarati; do not assume equal character timing. The player uses media playback time as the clock, not timers started on button tap. Highlighting pauses/resumes with audio, clears on cancellation, and advances to the next line on `ended`. A playback token prevents an old `ended` event from reading over a newer repeat or scene change. Test slow device/startup latency and rapid repeat/select interactions.

Before any paid batch, produce a credit worksheet from **unique** approved characters per language and the actual chosen model rate: `estimated credits = sum(unique characters × verified credits/character) + planned retakes`. Reserve a stated retake allowance, compare to the account's current remaining balance, and get explicit approval for the paid generation batch. Any previously observed balance must be rechecked; it is not a live budget. Save the estimate and actual debit per batch. No ElevenLabs credits are spent by this plan.

## 4. Runtime and offline Android package

Keep the existing HTML/CSS/JavaScript-in-WebView direction, but split the new implementation into modules: library/catalog, story graph/content, playback/cue controller, character rig/animation, scene renderer, parent settings, and per-story progress. Story IDs and asset IDs are data; no story-specific branching should be hardcoded in the player. Preload the next likely audio/scene assets conservatively. Package all fonts, art, cues, and narration in the APK (or another explicitly designed offline install package), with no network dependency. Startup, language switching, saved progress, and every branch must work in airplane mode.

Extend the Android asset handler to serve actual audio and font MIME types and byte ranges/seek behavior required by WebView media playback; the current handler only recognizes HTML/CSS/PNG and otherwise returns JavaScript. Verify playback from the installed APK, not just a browser file URL. Audit package size, decode memory, and load time before choosing compressed audio format/bitrate. Keep Android screen pinning as an opt-in parent action and verify the system prompt, unpin gesture, and parent-gate behavior on a physical phone. Android screen pinning is not a substitute for device-level parental controls.

## 5. Temporary asset register and replacement gates

Every temporary item gets an entry in a single checked-in register, keyed by the same asset/line ID used at runtime: `id`, `kind`, `whereUsed`, `currentRevision`, `TEMPORARY` or `FINAL_CANDIDATE` or `APPROVED`, `reason`, `replacementSpec`, `owner`, `reviewer`, `dueMilestone`, `evidenceLink`, `replacedBy`. Put `TEMPORARY` in source metadata/filename or manifest and show an obvious development-only placeholder badge in internal builds. Do not rely on a private to-do list or memory. The existing two PNGs, generated music/effects, and any prototype prose should enter the register if carried into new builds.

Gate A (vertical slice): one complete scene with both princess rigs and all required gestures, one scene background, and fully reviewed voice/timing in all three languages running offline on Android. Temporary items are allowed but listed in the build report. Gate B (complete adventure): every graph node reachable; all story, help, teaser, and UI lines localized and voiced; no unregistered assets; full branch/replay tests pass. Gate C (final-quality candidate): no `TEMPORARY` items in shipped content; all art/animation, translations, voices, timings, and sound mix approved by named reviewers with evidence. A build script/CI check should fail Gate C if any temporary record is unresolved or a required locale/audio/cue/asset is missing. The build summary must list remaining temporary entries even for earlier gates.

## 6. Test criteria and evidence

- **Content:** schema validation; no dangling targets, unreachable scenes, accidental infinite loops, single-Continue screens, untranslated strings, missing audio/cues, or endings without a replay/try-again route. Automated traversal covers every node and edge for each language and both princess settings.
- **Interaction:** first-open instruction; card tap/hold/speaker; choice tap versus hold/speaker separation; replay screen; help; cancellation when navigating; no overlapping narration; screen highlighting in sync after pause/resume; progress save/restore; parent language/princess switch. Include touch tests that catch a long press also triggering selection.
- **Visual/audio:** inspect representative and all unique gestures on a real portrait phone, including joint seams, blinking during speech, lip motion, crop/safe areas, Gujarati shaping, reduced motion, and sound balance. Native speakers listen to every generated Gujarati and Spanish line, not just a sample. Review highlighting against its audio at normal speed.
- **Android/offline:** signed APK install and launch, airplane-mode full route, background/foreground, rotation policy, storage persistence, cold-start timing, media seeking/ranges, screen pin/unpin, and Android version/device coverage. Report exact device, Android version, APK hash, test date, and which checks were browser-only or automated. An APK build and signature check alone do not establish phone behavior.
